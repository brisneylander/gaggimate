A steadier Silvia

This is my fork of GaggiMate, tuned on my own Rancilio Silvia. It's all about one thing: keeping the boiler at the temperature you asked for, before, during and after a shot. Here's the problem it fixes, how it goes about it, and the measurements to back it up.

First, a bit of background

If you already know your way around GaggiMate and PID loops, skip ahead. If not, here's what you need for the rest of the page to make sense.

What GaggiMate is

GaggiMate is an open-source kit that replaces the simple on/off thermostat in machines like the Gaggia Classic and Rancilio Silvia. It adds a touchscreen, shot profiles, pressure and flow control, and, most importantly here, proper electronic control of the boiler heater.

How it controls temperature

A thermocouple (a temperature sensor) is screwed to the boiler. Several times a second a PID controller compares that reading with your target and decides how hard to run the heater: more when it's cold, less as it gets close. It's the same idea as cruise control in a car.

The catch: the sensor is late

On a Silvia the thermocouple sits on the outside of the boiler. Heat from the element, or a rush of cold water, takes about 20 seconds to show up there. So the controller is always reacting to what the boiler was doing 20 seconds ago.

Think of a shower that takes 20 seconds to respond to the tap. You turn it hotter, nothing happens, so you turn it more… and then it's scalding. That's exactly what a plain PID loop does to a Silvia's boiler.

What that looks like in real life

You've probably seen some of these on your own machine without knowing why:

Overshoot after a flush

Flush some water and the reading dips. The controller piles on heat, but by the time the sensor notices, there's far too much on the way. The boiler sails up to 95–96 °C and takes 5 minutes to settle back.

The scary dip on your shot graph

During a shot the graph often falls 3–5 °C. Some of that is real, because cold tank water is coming in, but a lot of it is just the slow sensor. More on that below.

Waiting ages after steaming

Steaming leaves the boiler at 110–120 °C. Getting back to brew temperature means guessing how much to flush, or waiting a long time for it to cool.

What the fork does about it

Everything below is off until you turn it on, so an unconfigured machine behaves like normal GaggiMate.

Accounts for the slow sensor

The fork keeps track of the heat it has already put in and the cold water that's gone through, but which the sensor hasn't noticed yet. The PID works on where the temperature is heading, not where the sensor says it is, so it eases off before it overshoots.

Overshoot +2.9 → +0.6 °C

Heats for the water during a shot

While water is flowing, the heater adds power for the cold water coming in straight away, instead of waiting for the reading to drop. GaggiMate calls this feedforward (the "Kf" setting).

Smaller dip on the same draw

Flush to target

After steaming, press flush once. The fork works out exactly how much tank water will bring the boiler down to brew temperature, and flushes just that.

From 115 °C to 93 in about 1½ min

Knows the group head is cold

The big brass group head soaks up heat for the first half hour. The fork estimates how warm it is and heats a bit harder to make up for it, so warm-ups sag less.

Warm-up sag halved

Knows the tank is warming up

The water tank sits next to the boiler and warms from about 25 °C to 40 °C over a few hours. The fork estimates that, so flush to target uses the right water temperature.

Spots a missed refill

A heater safety cut-out

If the heater runs flat out and the reading doesn't move (say the thermocouple has come loose), or the reading jumps about, the heater is switched off until a restart.

No false stops since 4 November

The headline result: a 5 second flush

The simplest test there is. Hold at 93 °C, flush for 5 seconds, and watch what the heater does afterwards. Normal GaggiMate keeps heating into a dip that's already over, then overshoots. The fork lands close to 93 and settles in well under half the time.

Things I learned along the way

A few findings that are useful whichever firmware you run.

Don't trust the shot graph's dip

When the pump stops, the reading climbs back 3–4 °C in under a minute with the heater almost off. That heat was already in the boiler; the sensor just hadn't caught up. Someone on the GaggiMate Discord measured inside the basket and saw it stay within about 1.2 °C of target while the graph dipped 5 °C.

Give it half an hour

Even when the screen says 93 °C, a shot 10–20 minutes after switching on dips about twice as far as one later on. The group head is still soaking up heat. About 30 minutes after switch-on you're fine.

More isn't always better

Higher PID gains, more feedforward, or blasting full power after a flush all looked good on paper, but they either didn't help or caused more overshoot. The heat is still on its way when the reading catches up.

Getting it on your machine

You need a GaggiMate display and controller already fitted. The fork follows upstream GaggiMate closely (La Simona 7 includes everything up to 12 November, setup wizard and all), so the hardware and wiring are exactly the same.

  1. Flash it once over USB

    Normal GaggiMate only updates itself from GaggiMate, so the first install has to be over USB: flash the display and controller files from the latest release. After that, updates arrive over Wi-Fi like normal.

  2. Pick an update channel

    In Settings → System & Updates: Stable gets the numbered La Simona releases, Nightly gets every change as it lands.

  3. Load the Silvia profile

    In Settings → Machine → Machine Boiler Profile, choose Rancilio Silvia 230 V and save. That fills in all the measured values in one go.

  4. Turn on flush to target

    Under Settings → General, switch on Flush to brew target when hot, so the flush button does the clever thing after steaming.

  5. Not a Silvia? Tune it

    Run Tune this machine under Settings → Calibration. It walks you through an autotune, one weighed flush and one flush after steaming, and measures your machine's own values.

Tested on one machine: a 230 V Silvia V4 with Rancilio's boiler insulation added. Insulated V5 and V6 Silvias should be close; uninsulated ones will need their own tuning.
