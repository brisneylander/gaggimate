A GaggiMate fork · tested on a Rancilio Silvia 230 V

Boiler control that knows its sensor is late

The Silvia's thermocouple sits on the outside of the boiler and sees heat about 20 seconds after it happens. This fork teaches GaggiMate's controller to account for that, then builds tools around it: flushing a hot boiler straight down to brew temperature, estimating the tank's temperature, and a safety layer for the heater.

The same 5 second flush from 93 °C, logged on the same machine. The stock loop keeps heating into a dip the sensor is still showing, then overshoots. With the fork's compensation it lands close to target and settles in less than half the time.

What's in it

Everything is off until configured, so an unconfigured machine behaves like upstream GaggiMate.

Lag compensation

A heat-balance predictor counts the heater energy and flush water that haven't reached the sensor yet, so the PID backs off before an overshoot shows. Heat and water get separate delays.

+2.9 → +0.63 °C overshoot

Flush to target

After steaming the boiler sits at 106–110 °C. The flush button works out how much tank water brings it to brew temperature and flushes exactly that.

Peaks 6 °C lower, lands without overshoot

Tank estimate

The tank warms beside the boiler, from about 25 °C fresh to 40 °C or more. The fork models that, resets on a refill, and checks itself against flushes.

Spots a missed refill from two flushes

Heater safety layer

One gate in front of the heater pin. It latches the heater off if the sensor comes loose, the reading jumps, or the boiler passes 170 °C.

Three guided steps

Getting it on your machine

For a GaggiMate display and controller. The fork follows upstream GaggiMate closely, so the hardware and wiring are the same.

  1. Flash the fork once over USB

    Upstream firmware only updates from upstream, so the first install is by USB: flash the display and controller files from the latest release. After that, updates come over the air.

  2. Pick an update channel

    In Settings → System & Updates: Stable installs tagged releases, Nightly follows the head of the fork's master branch.

  3. Apply a machine profile

    In Settings → Machine → Machine Boiler Profile, apply Rancilio Silvia 230 V, or a Gaggia or DeLonghi starting point, then save.

  4. Tune it

    On another machine, run Tune this machine under Settings → Calibration to measure its own values. Turn on Flush to brew target when hot under General to make the flush button use them.

Only the Silvia profile is measured. The Gaggia and DeLonghi entries are upstream's documented PID gains as a starting point, not tested here.

What was tried and dropped

Every idea was checked on the bench. These didn't earn their place.

Full-power reheat after a flushSlower back to target (124–129 s against 62–100 s): heat takes about 2 minutes to reach the sensor, so delivering it fast doesn't help.
Boost, then flush the surplus off366–375 s to settle against 74–84 s. Heating up takes as long as a plain recovery, then the flush starts another.
A thermal model steering the water estimateNo difference at the sensor (+0.69 against +0.66 °C). Any gain would be in the water, which needs a probe inside the boiler to see.
Keeping shot temperature up with the heaterA shot's fill draws about 2 kW against the element's 1100 W, so heating during the shot lifts the water only about 0.6 °C. Set the profile a little hotter instead.
